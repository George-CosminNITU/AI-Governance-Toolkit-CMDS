# AI Compliance Toolkit Architecture

**Version:** 1.0  
**Phase:** Year 1, Semester 2 Deliverable  

## Overview
The proposed AI Compliance Toolkit is a comprehensive governance framework designed to help organizations evaluate, approve, and monitor the use of Generative AI technologies while effectively reducing compliance and security risks (such as "Shadow AI"). 

To ensure a structured approach to AI governance, the toolkit is built upon a four-layer architecture. This layered approach enables organizations to combine governance, compliance, and continuous monitoring within a single, cohesive framework.

## Layer 1: Policy Layer
The Policy Layer serves as the foundation of the framework. It defines the core rules and boundaries for AI usage across the organization.
* **Components:** 
  * The Acceptable Use Policy (AUP).
  * Data classification requirements (Public, Internal, Confidential).
  * The "White-list" of approved AI tools.
* **Objective:** To establish clear, non-negotiable guidelines for employees and third-party vendors regarding what is permissible when interacting with GenAI.

## Layer 2: Risk Assessment Layer
Before any AI tool is added to the approved list, it must pass through this evaluation layer to ensure technical and operational safety.
* **Components:** 
  * Risk identification protocols.
  * Vendor assessments (evaluating terms of service, data retention, and training policies).
  * Risk scoring processes (calculating Likelihood vs. Impact).
* **Objective:** To proactively evaluate AI tools and specific corporate use cases before they are deployed, ensuring data sovereignty and preventing intellectual property leakage.

## Layer 3: Compliance Layer
This layer acts as the legal safeguard, translating abstract regulatory requirements into actionable operational checks.
* **Components:** 
  * Alignment checks with the EU AI Act (e.g., transparency obligations).
  * Alignment checks with the GDPR (e.g., Article 22 restrictions on automated decision-making).
  * Maintenance of necessary documentation and formal approval procedures.
* **Objective:** To ensure that all AI usage remains strictly within the bounds of European and international law, minimizing the risk of administrative fines.

## Layer 4: Monitoring and Governance Layer
AI governance is not a one-time setup; models evolve, and new tools are constantly released. This layer ensures long-term framework sustainability.
* **Components:** 
  * Continuous oversight mechanisms.
  * Regular system audits and incident reporting channels.
  * Periodic reviews and dynamic policy updates.
* **Objective:** To provide continuous oversight, ensuring that the human-in-the-loop protocols are being followed and that the policy adapts to the rapidly changing technological landscape.
